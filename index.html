Why Ludo King stays on top

A game everyone already knows

Ludo comes from Pachisi, the royal race game played in India for centuries. The rules take a minute to learn, which is exactly why a match can include grandparents, cousins and first-timers at the same table — on one phone or across the world.

Luck you can argue with

The dice decide a lot, but not everything. Which token you move, when you take a risk, and where you choose to hide are real decisions. That blend of luck and light strategy is why a losing player is never truly out until the last token comes home.

How to Play Ludo King

Ludo is a race. Each player controls four tokens of one colour — red, green, yellow or blue. Your job is to move every token all the way around the board and up your home column into the centre triangle before anyone else does. Simple to say, gloriously chaotic to do.

Setting up

Choose your colour and mode. All four of your tokens start in your home yard (the big coloured corner). Players take turns rolling a single die. The youngest, luckiest or loudest player goes first — houses differ, the game does not mind.

The basic turn

Roll the die. On your turn, tap to roll. The number is how far one of your tokens may move.
Leave base with a six. A token can only enter the board from the yard when you roll a 6. It is placed on your starting square.
Move clockwise. Tokens travel around the outer track in the direction of the arrows, one square per pip.
Capture to send rivals home. Land exactly on an opponent's token (on a normal square) and it returns to its yard — and you earn an extra turn.
Climb the home column. After a full lap, turn into the coloured home column of your colour and head for the centre.
Finish with an exact roll. A token reaches the centre only on the exact number needed. Bring all four home to win.

Bonus turns

You roll again when you throw a six, when you capture an opponent, and when a token reaches home. Chaining these together is how big leads are built — and how a quiet game suddenly turns into a runaway.

Safe squares explained

The squares marked with a star, together with each player's starting square, are safe squares. A token resting there cannot be captured. Two tokens of the same colour on one square form a block: opponents cannot pass or capture it. Use safe squares as rest stops on long journeys across enemy territory.

Winning the game

The first player to bring all four tokens into the centre wins the match. In multiplayer games the remaining players can keep playing for second and third place. In Quick mode, the player with the most tokens home — or furthest advanced — when time or turns run out takes the win, depending on the room settings.

Tips & Tricks: Win More Ludo King Matches

You cannot control the dice. You can control what every roll is worth. These are the habits that separate lucky players from consistently good ones.

1. Leave base early — but not blindly

A token in the yard scores nothing. When you roll a six early, bring a new token out rather than inching an advanced token forward — more tokens on the board means more options on every later roll. The exception: if the token you would move can reach a safe square or score a capture, that value usually beats a fresh start.

2. Spread your tokens out

Four tokens bunched together give you one plan. Tokens spread around the board give you four. With pieces at different distances, almost any number the dice give you becomes useful somewhere.

3. Count the squares behind you

Before you move, glance six squares behind the token you are about to leave. If an enemy sits within dice range and your token is not on a safe square, assume it can be captured. Sometimes the best move is the one that runs away, not the one that runs forward.

4. Live on safe squares

Plan routes that pause on star squares, especially when crossing in front of an opponent's start. A token on a safe square is untouchable — treat those squares as hotels on a long and dangerous road.

5. Build blocks at choke points

Two of your tokens on the same square form a block nobody can pass or capture. Park a block just before an opponent's home column or outside their start and you slow their whole game while your other tokens race on.

6. Capture with purpose

Capturing feels great and earns a bonus roll — but chasing a capture that leaves your own token stranded in front of three enemies is a trade you will regret. Capture when you can land safe, land home, or send back the leader's most advanced token.

7. Protect the leader, feed the stragglers

Your most advanced token is everyone's target. Move it in safe-square hops near the end. Meanwhile, do not forget a token still near the start — a forgotten token at the finish line costs more games than any bad roll.

8. Play the endgame by exact numbers

In the home column, small rolls matter. Keep tokens staggered at different distances from the centre so that rolls of 1 through 6 all have a home. If every token needs the same number, the dice will happily refuse to give it.

9. Take the risk when you are behind

Losing players should gamble: leave safe squares, chase captures, split blocks. Winning players should do the opposite. The correct amount of risk depends entirely on the scoreboard, not on courage.

10. In Quick mode, tempo is everything

Short games reward early aggression. Get tokens out fast, capture often, and do not spend turns perfecting a defence the clock will never let anyone test.
